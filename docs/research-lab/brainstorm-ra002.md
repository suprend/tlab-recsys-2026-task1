## RA2-01 — discover leaves through prefix-conditioned identifier paths

**Paper-owned mechanism.** [SEATER, 2024 revision and SIGIR-AP2024 publication](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.13375v2), first preprint2023, builds a balanced identifier tree from trained SASRec item embeddings and generates paths with autoregressive decoding. No titles are required. The [author model](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ethan00Si/SEATER_Generative_Retrieval/main/model/SEATER.py) restricts each expansion to current-node children, adds sibling log-probabilities to path scores and returns terminal product IDs. Its [tree builder](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ethan00Si/SEATER_Generative_Retrieval/main/utils/build_tree.py) assumes ready embeddings and uses constrained clustering with `n_jobs=-2`; temporal provenance and our resource limits require a separate wrapper.

For a valid leaf path `c1…cD`, score `Σ_l log p(c_l | history,c_<l)`. Prefix-conditioned cross-attention determines the visited leaves. This differs from HYP004/08’s one user vector followed by flat item dot/ANN; a category quota does not reproduce conditional probabilities along learned, item-addressing paths. It neither looks up another user’s labeled prefix nor fires an exact observed itemset. [ETEGRec2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05546v1) supplies a newer end-to-end tokenizer/recommender direction, but counts as the same broad family, not another novelty ID.

**Our bounded adaptation.** Use only product IDs and event types, pooled within whole timestamp buckets; order exists between distinct seconds. Build one frozen tree over a declared past-observed source catalogue V. New products without a trained address are source-OOV, even if U(t) already knows them. Do not extend the dictionary using future IDs, force missing targets into leaves, or remove previously bought products. Same-second order permutations must leave candidates unchanged.

The first control is **exhaustive legal-leaf path scoring using the same fitted decoder/tree**, on a tiny catalogue: it isolates beam approximation without retraining. A later flat classifier control uses the same encoder inputs, item support, mature training queries and fit ceiling. Its loss/parameterization differs, so any quality comparison is a whole retrieval procedure contrast, not proof that every gain came from beam search.

**Cheapest falsifier.** Four leaves: root masses A=.6,B=.4; children under A=.5/.5, under B=.99/.01. Leaf probabilities are .30,.30,.396,.004. Beam1 selects A and misses b1; beam2 must recover b1. Correct sibling normalization, accumulated path scores and legal-child masks are essential. A second dictionary fixture has valid codes00→a,01→b,10→c:11 is invalid/underfill. Equal codes for distinct products require an explicit disambiguating suffix or ambiguity failure. Product-ID ties are technical; they do not establish action order. This proves that branch mass and best-leaf mass differ, not that learned paths improve purchases.

**Time and cost.** Upstream embedding fit ends at c0; tree completion/availability at c1 precedes every downstream decoder-training anchor s; those anchors’ whole windows satisfy `s+24h≤c2`; decoder fit/build finishes and is available before quality query t0. A late tree cannot be backdated into earlier training rows or caches. Cache identity binds history cutoff/version, tree, item mapping, decoder and search settings; user_id alone is insufficient.

Proposed finite admission:≤1m source rows, V≤20k IDs, d64, branching≤8,≤6 scored expansion levels **including any terminal/EOS level**,≤120001 nodes, one clustering initialization,≤20 iterations/node and an inclusive30CPUmin build cap; solver work also counts. Source rows/catalogue must cover the declared reservation and as-of inventory proxy; exceeding a cap stops admission, rather than silently choosing a different source sample. No dense item×item matrix. Query beam32/output20 allows at most `32×8×6=1536` child-score slots, **plus** history encoding, decoder/cache copying and top-k work. Zero-child nodes fail explicitly. Longer paths, retries or flat-search refill do not rescue underfill. The tree and upstream embedding fits are not free just because the paper assumes another deployed model already exists.

Counterhypothesis: sparse histories cannot learn useful branch conditionals; balanced clustering shares nuisance popularity, and beam pruning loses rare purchases. Defer quality testing unless the literal exact-leaf oracle passes and a later cost gate shows legal leaf coverage and complete resource accounting.

## RA2-02 — reverse discrete item replacement using conditional preference ratios

**Paper-owned mechanism.** [PreferGrow, NeurIPS2025, §§3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26063v1) perturbs the preferred item by discrete replacement, learns reference-conditioned log-ratios, then reverses the process. Its closed-form family uses an idempotent replacement matrix. The [active PairWise implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hugo-Chinn/PreferGrow/main/graph_lib.py), around line640, computes one O(N) reverse-ratio vector; it does not require storing N² ratios. [Sampling code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hugo-Chinn/PreferGrow/main/sampling.py) starts from a non-preference state and returns a final all-item vector. Four predictor steps with final denoising cost **five** all-catalogue score passes.

**Our adaptation.** Fix `E=11ᵀ/N`, including self replacement, on the frozen legal V. Then `E²=E` and `P_α=αI+(1−α)E`. Forbid no-self rejection sampling: `(J−I)/(N−1)` is a different, non-idempotent E. Sample alternatives are unobserved items, not verified dislikes. This artificial corruption clock is distinct from event time, purchase intervals and recurrence.

Train on mature daily-window purchases, assigning each covered distinct positive weight1/|covered positives| within a query, and report uncovered-positive mass separately. Preserve empty-Y queries in evaluation. This categorical training distribution expresses relative item mass conditional on observed purchase support; it does not estimate whether the user buys or a calibrated probability of every next24h purchase.

At inference initialize one reference ID from V using a frozen query seed independent of Y, take four fixed reverse steps at integrated-noise levels `(6,4.5125,3.025,1.5375,.05)` and one final denoising pass to0, then top20 from that final vector. No repeated sampling until20 unique products. This is our fixed schedule; record initialization/discretization approximation and never evaluate a singular inverse at α=0. For uniform E, initializing from uniform at `α_min=exp(−6)` has pre-reversal distribution error≤α_min in total variation; that does not bound errors after learned reversal.

Novelty is **reference-conditioned reverse dynamics**, not another monotone graph walk or a new label for SASRec. However, one-shot classification might learn the same ordering more cheaply. Use the same conditional encoder, item table, catalogue, mature queries and training ceiling for ratio and direct categorical-classifier procedures. A separate same-checkpoint diagnostic compares iterative versus one-step denoising at declared noise endpoints. Different scoring work remains reported: five passes versus one is not equal latency simply because both have the same output quota. If reference conditioning/iteration does not alter legal admissions, reject the incremental mechanism.

**Cheapest falsifier.** N=3, α=.5, target distribution p0=(.8,.15,.05), reference b. Bayes reversal is `p0_i P_α[b,i]/Σ_j p0_j P_α[b,j]` =(.551724,.413793,.034483); forward replacement from b is(.166667,.666667,.166667). Swapping a/c mass swaps the reverse preference while leaving the forward process unchanged. Check column/row orientation, sums and finite nonnegative weights. Also supply inconsistent estimated ratios that make the inverse produce negative mass: it must fail explicitly. Silent clipping would be a new adaptation needing its own comparison.

Do not use author `get_one_step_score` as a generator: it accepts x0 and corrupts/scorers that supplied item. Query initialization must not depend on the current positive. Author variable names such as `logits` do not make reverse weights calibrated probabilities. The primary paper’s all-item complexity and modeling difficulty remain relevant limitations, not evidence of cheapness.

**Time and bounds.** If an encoder is pretrained, it is available before all ratio/classifier-training anchors; each training window ends before its fit cutoff. Parameters, mapping, V and RNG/search configuration become available before quality queries. Freeze V for the block; later known products remain source misses. Any history cache has to be actually implemented, paid and keyed by cutoff/checkpoint.

Proposed future ceiling: same≤1m admitted source rows/V≤20k/d64 and≤4000 mature training queries; one trajectory, batch1, five full score vectors/query,≤100000 scored item slots/query and≤10m for100Q. Neural multiply-accumulate and training costs are additional and billed. O(N) scratch does not imply cheap learning or all N² relations learned for free. Fail on nonfinite/negative reverse masses, exhausted time/memory, missing source coverage or unsupported model state. This has lower priority than RA2-01 because even the bounded adaptation needs learned-ratio and numerical validation.
