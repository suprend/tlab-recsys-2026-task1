## Common prospective contract

Use only product/category IDs, event types, timestamps, user/session and available category path/brand/price. No titles, images, impressions, order IDs or inferred stock. U(t) contains prior-observed products. At every global second t, predict before ingesting **any** event at t. A fixed-clock query knows the latest observed `(user,session)` prefix and its age, not a current or completed basket. Same-second events form unordered sets; ID order is only an output tie-break. A session identifier crossing users never merges their histories.

Warmup fit cutoff, label maturity cutoff, build completion and `available_at` are separate. Encoders, priors, routing weights and any codebook/index must exist before the downstream block; training rows need their own historical prefixes, not the graph at training-end. Event-attached historical metadata stays historical; candidate metadata uses the latest global bucket<t, with known+NULL/conflicts ambiguous and no clean older backoff. Missing/OOV/unsupported differs from an observed zero. A last-session source uses only its observed seven-day prefix; a crossing timestamp bucket is discarded whole with everything older when enforcing a distinct-item cap.

One possible future comparison preserves the P140 and R20 sources and replaces **global ranks141–200** with at most60 mechanism candidates. The control supplies those original60 ranks. Both deduplicate without refill. Predeclare source-depth, quota ownership, score/ID ties and an interleaved admission order before labels. Report native lists and size-matched prefixes of length `m_q=min(|C_control|,|C_treatment|)`. Empty mechanism support leaves underfill; no extra source, deeper search or positive insertion rescues it. Current protocol changes require their own version; this note grants none.

## RB1 — allocate a finite quota from interest mass, not equal category slots

[SPARC, Shi et al., 2025 preprint, §3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09090v1#S3.SS5) predicts a distribution over discrete interest codes, selects multiple codes, creates code-conditioned user vectors and runs parallel retrieval; it explicitly permits probability-based quota allocation. The paper's offline evaluation includes a positive plus99 sampled negatives (§4.1.2). Its results therefore do not establish our full-Y retrieval gain or probability calibration; its industrial metrics are a different objective.

**Proposed mechanism:** given one frozen interest distribution and three fixed ordered item lists, allocate60 slots by largest-remainder rounding of predicted mass, versus fixed20/20/20 and hard top-interest60. First use literal category IDs/list probabilities to test allocation; a learned codebook is deferred. This allocation contrast is the novelty; replacing categories with learned clusters alone is not. HYP053 already covers category quotas, and HYP068 already covers max versus mean interest scores.

**Separating case:** mass `(0.8,0.1,0.1)` allocates `(48,6,6)` rather than `(20,20,20)`; mass `(0.34,0.33,0.33)` should remain spread. Identical category membership and item ranks can produce different admissions solely through quota mass. Counterhypothesis: mass is miscalibrated, dominant interests consume all useful slots, or overlap eliminates the nominal difference. Entropy is uncertainty about predicted interest distribution, not epistemic confidence or a license to increase total C.

**Cheapest falsifier/budget unit:** three literal lists, two distributions and≤12 distinct IDs;0fits/0Y. Check exact rounding, tie and exhausted-list expectations, all-overlap and zero-history fallback. Reject this recipe if it cannot yield the specified nontrivial contrast within the same60 slots. Future learned testing must charge probability estimation, every list lookup and duplicate, not just admitted IDs;≤3 search paths and60 emitted source rows/query are initial caps, not measured runtime.

## RB2 — conjunctive prefix completion beyond all pairwise projections

[NPA, Luo et al., 2024, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16433v1#S3) identifies item-combination patterns through vector-quantized attention and derives context describing missing components. It supports multiple granularities/intent channels and any-order training. Its basket/playlist completion setup, random partial-basket splits and exclusion of present basket items are **not** our fixed-clock next24h purchase task.

**Proposed mechanism:** a bounded exact surrogate retrieves item i from support of `(prefix subset S ∪ {i})`, conditioned on at least two distinct prefix products. Build observed session sets, explicitly labelled proxies; no claim that they are orders, live cart state or complements. Keep repeated products admissible in the overall pipeline. A cheap set-count screen tests whether higher-order support deserves a later NPA-like representation fit, not whether the neural codebook itself helps.

**Separating fixture:** four3-item sets A=`abc,ade,bdf,cef`; B=`abd,ace,bcf,def`. Every item has degree2; every pair count is identical between A and B; every session degree is3. Consequently pairwise cosine and degree-normalized two-hop item projections coincide. Given `{a,b}`, observed three-way completion is c in A and d in B. A method retaining only the pairwise projection cannot recover this distinction. Generic hypergraph convolution is not automatically sufficient if it collapses to that projection.

**Cheapest falsifier/budget unit:** those eight literal sets,0fits/0Y; compare exact triple supports and permutation invariance. Also add a same-second pair at t and require no change to the prior query. Reject an implementation whose outputs stay pairwise-identical or that uses future members to finish the prefix. Counterhypothesis: real prefixes rarely contain repeated supported conjunctions, so lookup is empty or merely popularity. Future cost is retained set memberships plus attempted subset/item intersections; cap prefix10, subsets45 and source60/query, with separate global-build/key ceilings before source access. Exhaustion yields empty source, not pairwise refill.

## RB3 — item repeatability changes which history items deserve quota

[TREx, Li et al., SIGIR2024, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01143v1#S5) separates repeat and explore modules. Repeat scores combine user interest with an item-specific repurchase statistic; a threshold admits repeat candidates before exploration fills remaining places. The authors expressly question whether beyond-accuracy improvements reflect useful recommendations. Their threshold is a score threshold, not a calibrated posterior confidence bound.

**Proposed mechanism:** use a training-prefix item-repeatability prior, common user-frequency/recency support and one frozen threshold to allocate a bounded repeat quota; use the same explore list in its comparator. A simple prior is external users with at least two purchase-seconds divided by external users with at least one, on the same fixed past window; record both supports and exclude query-user contributions before division. This is a historical fraction, not probability of repurchasing. This is distinct from “recent items help” only if item-specific repeatability changes admission while the user's histories, support and decay remain fixed. Prior-any-event membership must not masquerade as prior-purchase repetition. Purchase-seconds/session proxies are not orders; left-censored October history limits the claim.

**Separating case:** products a and b have identical user counts and recencies, but external training-prefix repeatability priors0.8 and0.1; one repeat slot and threshold0.5 admit a. A second fixture swaps priors while keeping user history unchanged: admission must swap. This defeats a purely direct-history recency score. Counterhypothesis: the prior is popularity/support in disguise or technical resubmissions inflate repeatability.

**Cheapest falsifier/budget unit:**≤12 literal purchase records, two products and four users;0fits/0Y. Check exact duplicate rows/same-second purchases, own-user contribution policy, support-zero fallback and prior-swap. Do not copy the paper's low-support additive correction as calibrated shrinkage. Future estimation pays for all prior counters/refresh and every tested threshold; one threshold,≤20 repeat-item evaluations/query and one unchanged explore60-list are caps. No threshold grid is concealed inside a zero-fit pilot.

## RB4 — spend retrieval calls on useful seed/channel pairs

[CAPTS, 2026 preprint, §§3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12564v1#S3) credits a historical trigger by future engagement on its retrieved items, predicts channel-specific value and uniqueness, and combines recent online triggers with cached longer-history triggers. Its original signals include effective-view watch time and heterogeneous channels; we have neither watch-time supervision nor a pre-existing multi-channel serving stack. Learned value attribution requires mature historical outcomes, not future labels during inference.

**Proposed mechanism:** first screen duplicate-aware allocation over already specified history seeds and channel lists without fitting a new retriever. Compare latest-seed selection with a frozen greedy marginal-unique-yield policy, at identical seed×channel calls and list depth. This tests routing/computation allocation, not better ItemCF edges. A later learned downstream-value head is a separate, more expensive hypothesis; uniqueness alone does not prove relevance.

**Separating case:** h1 and h2 both return `{a,b}`, h3 returns `{c,d}`; two calls of depth2 yield two unique products for latest h1/h2 and four for h1/h3. Match actual admissions afterward and record both gained and displaced IDs; the example proves wasted calls, not quality gain. Counterhypothesis: duplicates concentrate relevance and unique-yield routing wastes scarce slots on irrelevant tails. Fixed-depth RRF cannot repair useful products absent from its seed-generated pool; fusion-only claims must hold that pool fixed.

**Cheapest falsifier/budget unit:** 3 seeds × 2 channels × depth2 literal cells, 0 fits/0Y; test complete overlap, disjoint lists, stale/missing cache and deterministic ties. A query at t may use only a cache completed before t; refreshing it from the later session invalidates the test. Future cap:≤20 seed×channel cells/query, depth≤10 each,≤200 examined IDs and source60 output; cache construction, stale refresh, routing and failures all count. Full learned CAPTS remains deferred until lawful historical supervision and equal train/fit/label budgets exist.

## Disposition

Prioritize RB2's pairwise-indistinguishable fixture, then RB4's call-allocation fixture; these can fail mechanically without consuming labels. Keep RB1 conditional on distinction from HYP053/068 and RB3 conditional on reliable repeat-support semantics. Each source-free fixture proposal is≤5CPUmin/512MiB/2threads and0fits/0target looks; none was executed here. A later real-source gate would need independent source/prefix/candidate checks, explicit global scan/state ceilings and a separately reserved evaluation domain. A100-query/220-output cap alone does not bound a full graph, prior or cache build. Paper success is motivation; current October heldout is closed and cannot become new confirmation.
