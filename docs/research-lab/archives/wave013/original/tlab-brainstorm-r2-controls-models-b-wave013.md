## Fixed C is a limitation, not an arm imbalance

C is identical for B/R2, so a changed candidate pool is not an explanation based on unfair arm inputs. R2 cannot insert off-C purchases or alter their denominator. Yet any gain is conditional on this retrieval task. For positive query q, let `r_q=IDCG20(Y∩C)/IDCG20(Y)` and `a_q=DCG20/IDCG20(Y∩C)` when covered targets exist. Then fullY NDCG is `r_q*a_q`; with no covered target it is0. Both arms share r_q, so their difference is `r_q*Δa_q`.

A literal two-query illustration has one covered positive whose rank moves2→1, and one positive outside C whose score stays0 in both arms. FullY macro gain is `(1−1/log2(3))/2=.18453512`, not a recovery of the off-C target. Different later query/coverage mixtures can change pooled gain even if the covered-query ordering effect is unchanged. This does not invalidate the metric or the task; it limits claims about general retrieval or preference. No new pool is proposed or inspected.

The prior dedup already excludes D(item,purchase,168h,user_id), because B includes the fixed retrieval popularity score with that meaning. R2's purchase24h and view24h distinct counts are not that exact alias. They may encode freshness/source-related popularity. Such an interpretation remains a counterhypothesis, not an observed explanation from aggregate scores.
