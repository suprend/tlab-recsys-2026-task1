## B. FullY normalization can change the preferred order even without zeros

**Dedup:** REFINE/MERGE existing TLAB-006/008/013/019, round072, wave028 full-IDCG refinement and MA-03/M001-C2 normalization discussion. This is a binary restricted-C witness, not another listwise loss or an admission.

**Mechanism / falsifiable prediction:** let D(m)=sum of 1/log2(r+1), r=1…min(20,m). Five equally weighted, observationally indistinguishable query worlds share C={a,b}; two have Y={a}, three Y={b,c,d,e}, with c,d,e outside C. Item features distinguish a/b but cannot reveal the world. Independent item-constant BCE optima are p(a)=2/5 and p(b)=3/5, preferring b. Expected normalized gains are 2/5 versus 3/(5D(4)), preferring a. The expected NDCG advantage of a-first is (1−1/log2(3))·[2/5−3/(5D(4))]>0. Candidate-only IDCG would reverse that conclusion. This elementary restricted-list adaptation follows the normalization distinction in [Ravikumar–Tewari–Yang, Lemma4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ravikumar11a/ravikumar11a.pdf); their complete-list theorem is not a direct guarantee for our finite boosting pipeline.

Varying |C| adds another measure change: with unchanged covered positives, their query-balanced mass is |Y∩C|/|C|. Adding two bottom-ranked nonpositives changes 1/2 to 1/4 while leaving this toy's NDCG unchanged. **Counterhypothesis:** real conditional normalized relevance has the same order as BCE probabilities; richer features distinguish worlds; finite-model changes dominate. No real mismatch or harmful-negative finding is asserted.

**Minimum test, UNRUN:** 5 queries × 2 candidate policies (size2/4) × 2 supplied orders =20 query-metric evaluations, 4 aggregates, 30 candidate-policy memberships/weight records, 14 shared fullY memberships and 60 supplied score values; 0 fits. Verify both order preferences and preserved NDCG under bottom-negative expansion. Failure rejects the witness; success proves possible estimand divergence only, not a replacement loss's utility.
