## One substantive refinement: candidate-set-dependent confidence

**Mechanism.** V2's gap is raw boundary separation divided by the whole candidate score range. Adding a strictly worse table can switch escalation while preserving every existing table score, the top-π set, and all join statistics. With π=1 and exactly two unequal scores, normalization always produces gap 1, however tiny their difference. This is a concrete scale/candidate-set failure, beyond alias or XOR examples. Renaming may perturb an embedding; the paper rule supplies no quantitative perturbation bound, so a large normalized gap cannot certify stability to nominal/schema presentation changes.

**Counterhypothesis.** The gap is intentionally an adaptive cost heuristic, not calibrated probability; candidate-set dependence might correlate with useful escalation, and semantic-signal gating may exclude cryptic inputs. That would limit the claim, but does not remove the algebraic behavior when the semantic route is enabled. Actual renaming effects, predictive false negatives and cost savings remain UNKNOWN.

**Falsifiable prediction.** At τ=0.1, supplied rational cosine scores A=5001/10000 and B=1/2 yield normalized gap 1 and the no-LLM branch. Add C=−1/2 without altering A/B: gap becomes 1/10001 and the LLM branch. A remains the highest-scoring table. With a declared absolute perturbation bound ε=1/10000, the A/B ranking is uncertified because their raw gap 1/10000 is at most 2ε; admissible perturbations can reverse it.
