## Small analytic appendix

For the learner-class argument only, let two binary coordinates x,y have target XOR and z=(x−y)². One threshold z>1/2 represents XOR. An ensemble of depth-one trees on x,y has score a+bx+cy. Perfect XOR classification would require F00,F11<0 and F10,F01>0, impossible because F00+F11=F10+F01. A depth-two representation can remove this obstruction. This proves a capacity-dependent representational possibility, not greedy CatBoost convergence or an attainable REES46/B41 history. For y>0, a ratio split x/y>c similarly becomes the oblique boundary x>cy; x−y and xy yield diagonal and curved boundaries. Numeric border availability and finite precision can still obstruct any proposed single split.
