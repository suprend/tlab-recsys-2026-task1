## S086-C1 — composed feature-and-prefix selection has its own estimand

**Merge:** HYP-002 /TLAB-016, with TLAB-019 and optional013; existing rounds019/069 and waves053/065 already cover selection bias, fixed-recipe forward validation and matched-prefix attribution. This is an integration refinement, not a novel algorithm or a demand to replace accepted evidence.

**Mechanism.** A future automated procedure that selects both a feature bundle and its tree count must keep *both choices* inside its inner development stage. An outer evaluation of the already frozen W/B recipes estimates those recipes; it does not repeat, or estimate the variability of, the feature-search procedure. Conversely, choosing k separately for B and the already selected W cannot be relabelled as joint feature-and-tree optimization across all arms. Current historical fixed300 and later exploratory1189/403 results remain valid within their stated scopes; their final CI crossing0 and reused73-positive-Q/2400-dependent-look limitations remain unchanged.

[Cawley–Talbot2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) explain how variance of a finite-sample selection criterion permits overfitting the model-selection step. [Varma–Simon2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1397873/) distinguish evaluation of an optimized procedure from reporting the minimum of the criterion used for optimization. Applying that distinction to our two-stage feature/prefix pipeline is an inference, not a theorem about sparse temporal ranking.

**Counterhypothesis.** The legal bundles and preferred prefixes may be sufficiently stable that reselecting them changes nothing; a larger nested procedure could spend substantial compute while losing positive-event support. A single fresh origin would still not establish search-policy generalization across dates or datasets. Historical final targets cannot become fresh outer data.

**Falsifiable prediction.** In a composed controller, changing only outer Y must leave generated arms, every inner-selected k, chosen W, model bindings and the decision seal identical. Changing inner Y in a deliberately discriminating toy must change at least one choice. An injected read of outer Y before the decision seal must be rejected even when every underlying feature, ranking and prefix selector passes its own tests. This checks the boundary between components, not quality gain.

**Closest minimal experiment, UNRUN.** First specify an optional prospective composed policy; preserve historical policies. Use five literal score-table arms B/I1/I2/R1/R2, four complete prefixes and four inner plus four outer queries, two candidates each: at most160 query-prefix rankings/320 score cells. Include one full-Y target outside C; fixed product ties, recall guard and arm-failure/no-refill semantics remain explicit. Exercise existing pure selection routines through doubles, seal the chosen program/prefix before revealing outer targets, then apply the inner/outer perturbations and an early-target-read mutant. No raw data, fit, CatBoost import or new heldout look. All outer tables are test fixtures, never inputs to selection.

**Proposed finite cap:** one synthetic CODE episode,35 CPU-seconds/50 wall-seconds,128MiB literal inputs,0ML; actual cost **UNKNOWN**, not granted. Accept only the invariance, deliberate responsiveness and mutant rejection together; otherwise retain the failure and repair the boundary. Passing proves composition mechanics only. Any real follow-up requires genuinely untouched chronological outer targets, mature train labels, fresh artifacts and separate explicit compute authority; evaluating the whole five-arm procedure would pay five full fits per origin plus all source/selection/oracle costs, not two frozen-recipe fits.

**Task1 value.** This makes “automatic feature selection with adaptive training” a precise, testable procedure while keeping the existing working automation deliverable moving. It prevents a future report from silently upgrading fixed-pipeline evidence into a claim about the search strategy.

**Rejected shortcut:** native `use_best_model` alone. [Official CatBoost fit docs](https://catboost.ai/docs/en/concepts/python-reference_catboost_fit#use_best_model) select the supplied eval metric; that is not automatically our full-Y ranking criterion. Native early stopping and post-fit prefix selection also have different compute costs. The existing external full-Y helper already addresses this distinction; no replacement is proposed.
