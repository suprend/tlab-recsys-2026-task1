## One refinement: where recovered targets land matters

**Existing owners:** HYP-061/062 retrieval; TLAB-004/006/007 diagnosis; wave073 ceiling and wave084 REFINE-A. HYP-087/088 remain reserved. This connects existing mechanisms, without a new algorithm or ID.

**Mechanism/hypothesis.** Under the same candidate budget, an alternative source can recover positives preferentially in queries with few retrieved positives, while displacing positives from already well-covered queries. Equal net target-pair hits can therefore produce different positive-query macro NDCG ceilings. A standalone source Recall or low Jaccard is insufficient to choose the source; measure final gained/lost pairs and their query-level ceiling increments. This is a hypothesis about useful source complementarity, not evidence that ItemCF has it.

For full binary Y and fixed C, let m=|Y|>0, h=|Y∩C|, G(n)=Σ[r=1…n]1/log₂(r+1). Oracle U=G(min(K,h))/G(min(K,m)). Existing stage accounting becomes **1−NDCG=(1−U)+(U−NDCG)** per query. Average on the unchanged mature-positive cohort, including empty-C queries. The first term is the fixed-C ceiling loss; the second is reordering headroom. Neither predicts a realizable improvement. Catalog-unavailable targets remain in m; catalog misses and source misses can be separately diagnosed using the existing U(t) proxy.

**Primary-source support and limit.** [Huang et al., arXiv v1, §§3.1/3.2/5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16080v1) study fusion of fixed channel lists and context-dependent allocations, evaluating set precision/recall/F1. This supports inspecting allocation separately from generating a new model, but does not establish purchase-NDCG gains here. Their normalized rounded quotas and deduplicated union do not, by the displayed equation alone, ensure equal *actual unique* cardinality. Our existing matched-size/refill controls therefore remain necessary. The NDCG consequence below is our derivation, not a result reported in that paper.
