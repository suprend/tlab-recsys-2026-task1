**Mechanism.** For binary targets let I(m)=Σ[r=1..min(20,m)]1/log2(r+1), m=|Yq| and c=|Yq∩Cq|. With m,c>0, wq=I(c)/I(m) and NDCGfull=wq·NDCGC for every permutation of the same C. Therefore any identical pair-swap numerator divided by full IDCG equals wq times its candidate-normalized counterpart. Under a linear lambda construction, both gradient and Hessian coefficients scale by wq. This is normalization, not recovery of unreachable targets or additional predictive information.

**Primary limits.** LambdaLoss connects metric-dependent pair weights to surrogates; its benchmark gains do not establish our sparse-purchase benefit. [Wang et al.,CIKM2018,Eq6/§6.2](https://marc.najork.org/papers/cikm2018.pdf) LightGBM v4.6.0 builds IDCG from candidate labels, then optionally applies score-distance adjustment and log2(1+Σλ)/Σλ normalization; row weights are applied afterward. Thus direct denominator replacement and post-hoc wq are generally unequal with nonlinear normalization. Set normalization false in both conceptual arms, or specify the actual transformed objective. Native truncation/pair numerators also need separate scrutiny; normalizer alignment alone does not make native loss exact NDCG20. [Pinned author code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/LightGBM/v4.6.0/src/objective/rank_objective.hpp)

**Prediction / counterexample.** Identical one-positive/one-negative candidate lists with fullY sizes1 and2 have identical candidate-normalized lambdas but full-Y coefficient ratio1/(1+1/log2(3)). If c=m, or both exceed20, wq=1 and this mechanism disappears. If c=0, all candidate labels are zero: pairwise signal stays zero despite fullY-positive evaluation. EmptyC,zeroY,singleton/all-positive groups remain in manifests; no target insertion or query filtering follows. Reweighting may hurt finite-model sharing; matching metric units guarantees no quality improvement.
