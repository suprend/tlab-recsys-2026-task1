## Primary sources: what transfers and what does not

[Li, Wang and McAuley, TiSASRec, WSDM2020, original author-hosted paper](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~jmcauley/pdfs/wsdm20b.pdf), §3.2, retains the most recent n actions and models pairwise time intervals alongside position. It scales intervals using a user's minimum positive interval and clips them. §3.4 explicitly discusses equal timestamps and retains positions. This motivates keeping event position and elapsed time separate; it does **not** authenticate a same-second order in REES46 or establish its 24-hour purchase-ranking benefit. Whole-sequence normalization is not imported: any future fitted scale must be available before its downstream anchor.

[Anderson, Kumar, Tomkins and Vassilvitskii, The Dynamics of Repeat Consumption, WWW2014, original author-hosted paper](https://cs.stanford.edu/~ashton/pubs/repeat-consumption-www2014.pdf), abstract/§4.1–4.2, models repeat choice using weights indexed by intervening consumptions and sums contributions from prior occurrences. It conditions on a repeat choice rather than solving whether a novel item is chosen. Its action-index clock is different from seconds. Neither fitted decay, repeat-only outcome conditioning nor its domain results are transferred to our all-query full-Y ranking task.

## C090-R1 — count after the latest candidate timestamp, returning the admissible event-lag interval

**Mechanism.** For q=(u,t), fixed non-NULL candidate i, immutable authenticated observed source prefix `[S,t)`, and view capability, define

`tau(u,i,t) = max{s : S≤s<t, user=u, product=i, action=view}`.

If absent, both outputs are NULL and missing=true. Otherwise let

`L = # raw view rows with user=u and tau<event_time<t`,

`M = # raw view rows with user=u, event_time=tau, product distinct from i (including NULL product)`,

`U = L+M`.

Units are **logged view-row instances**, not unique products, sessions, exposure or orders. Candidate-view rows at tau do not contribute to M; repeated copies of the candidate at tau do not occur after its latest possible occurrence. With no known within-timestamp order, the number of view rows after the most recent occurrence of i can range from L through U over the timestamp-consistent total orders. Hence `[L,U]` is a finite combinatorial range, not a confidence interval, arrival bound or estimate of hidden activity. It is exact only for the admitted observed bag and the stated partial order. A proposed last-N membership is certainly true if U<N, certainly false if L≥N, and UNKNOWN otherwise; N counts raw view rows and the latest candidate occurrence itself occupies one of the N positions. These predicates are a bridge to last-N semantics, not a threshold search or an extra output family.

Chronology: issue all q at t before applying **any** global timestamp-t event. Snapshot tau only from `<t`. At an older bucket tau, counting all rows first before storing the user cumulative snapshot avoids input-row-order dependence. Event-time availability is inherited as an assumption, not upgraded to received-at evidence. No future last-event, completed-session end, inactivity-defined future boundary, future anchor or target identifies a lower boundary. The interval from observed tau to t lies within the observed prefix; an old/missing pre-S occurrence is not imputed. Known source gaps would require a separately declared observed-bag interpretation or refusal of full-activity semantics, not zero filling.

**Duplicate policy.** Preserve raw multiplicity exactly as current history. A repeated noncandidate view at s>tau increases both bounds by1; at s=tau it increases U by1 only. A duplicate candidate view at tau changes neither bound. Global duplication may change the feature; that is an explicit counterhypothesis about logging intensity. There is no reliable order/transaction/dedup provenance that permits collapsing rows and calling the result unchanged semantics. A DISTINCT-product clock would be another declared operator with paid state and a separate comparison; it is not silently substituted here.

Only view/view is proposed, not a free9-binding action cross-product.
