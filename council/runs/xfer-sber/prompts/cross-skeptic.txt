You are the skeptic of a feature council for a ranking model. Reply with JSON only. You judge
novelty: a card is new only if its signature (mechanism + predicted stratum + distinguishing kill_test) differs

TASK
Orders of a grocery delivery service. Every anchor A is a midnight. A query is a user with at least one order
before A and at least one order in [A, A + 14 days). The target of a query is the set of distinct products of the
user's first order in that window (earliest ts, then smallest order_id); products the user bought before count too.
The label of a (user, product) candidate pair is whether the product is in the target. The metric is MAP@50:
AP@50 = (1 / min(50, M)) * sum over positions k = 1..50 of rel_k * P@k, where M is the number of target products
(targets outside the candidates included), rel_k is 1 when the product at position k is a target, and P@k is the
number of targets among the first k positions divided by k; MAP@50 is the mean AP@50 over all queries.

CANDIDATES (policy S-R150-P100-G200)
Up to 200 candidates per query, all statistics from the history before A.
Main retailer of the user: the retailer with the most distinct orders of the user; ties by the later last order of the user there, then by retailer code.
(a) repeat: the distinct products the user bought before A, by the number of distinct orders with the product descending, then the time of the latest such order descending, then product_id; the first 150.
(b) pop-retailer: products by distinct buyers at the main retailer in [A - 28 days, A) descending, then product_id; the first 100.
(c) global: products by distinct buyers over all orders in [A - 28 days, A) descending, then product_id.
Union: (a) in its order, then (b) without (a), then (c) without (a) and (b), until exactly 200 candidates. Source ranks repeat_rank, popr_rank and glob_rank are positions in the full orders.

TRAINING
CatBoost Logloss, 500 trees, depth 8, learning rate 0.05, L2 10, 128
borders, no tuning. Training anchors are 14 days apart; each contributes at most 20000 queries: all positive pairs
plus 20 hash-sampled negatives per query, with inverse-inclusion weights. Candidates are ranked by the raw
model value, ties by product_id.

Ideas of that agent, all of them in the model above (name: idea):
  p_next_order_reorder: Item-level chance that a buyer's next order contains the product again (smoothed), plus the median days between consecutive purchases of the product by the same user, over the last 90 days.
  user_repeat_fraction: How habitual the user is: mean share of products in each order (after the first in the 120-day window) that the user had already bought earlier in the window, plus the same share for the latest order.
  p_new_buyer_share: Smoothed share of the product's buyers in the last 28 days who had not bought it earlier in the 120-day window, separating trial-driven products from staples.
  user_brand_affinity_v2: Share of the user's orders in the 120-day window that contain the product's brand, and days since the user last bought that brand; the product brand is taken from all lines, so the value does not depend on the batch.
  up_decayed_frequency: Recency-weighted purchase frequency of the product by the user (sum over distinct orders of exp(-age/14 days)), raw and normalised by the user's own decayed order count.
  category_choice_v2: Within the product's category (taken from all lines, so batch-independent), the share of the user's category orders that picked this product, and whether the user's latest category order picked it (substitution vs loyalty).
  up_order_count_due: Number of the user's orders since the product was last bought, and that count divided by the user's typical number of orders per purchase of the product (order-based dueness instead of day-based).
  p_buyer_repeat_penetration: Item-level share of later orders (after the first) of multi-order buyers in the last 90 days that contain the product again, smoothed: how much of a buyer's ongoing basket the product occupies.
  up_adoption_streak: For products the user already bought in the 120-day window: the share of the user's orders since the first purchase of the product that contain it (persistence after adoption), and the number of consecutive latest orders that contain it (current streak).
  store_popularity: Popularity of the product at the user's main store (finer than retailer) in the last 28 days: share of the store's buyers who bought it, and its smoothed lift over the global buyer share.
  product_promo_price: Current promotion signal of the product: share of its lines with a positive discount in the last 7 days, and the median line price in the last 7 days relative to the median price in the 83 days before.
  product_category_dominance: How dominant the product is inside its category over the last 28 days: its distinct buyers divided by the category's distinct buyers, and its buyer rank within the category.
  user_category_due: Category-level replenishment timing for every candidate: days since the user last bought the product's category divided by the user's mean interval between category orders, and the share of the user's last 5 orders containing the category.
  up_rebuy_recency_bucket: Pseudo-label rate learned at A-14d: among users who had last bought the product in the same days-since-last bucket (0-7, 7-14, 14-28, 28-56, 56+) as this user now, the share whose first order in the next 14 days contained it, smoothed toward the bucket-wide rate.
  p_pseudo_adopt_rebuy14: Pseudo-label rates at A-14d over users with prior history: the share of prior non-buyers whose first order in the last 14 days adopted the product, and the smoothed share of prior buyers whose first order rebought it.
  uc_rebuy_recency_bucket: Category-level pseudo-label rate at A-14d: share of users with the same days-since-last-category-purchase bucket (or never bought the category) whose first order in the next 14 days contained the category, applied to every candidate via the user's current bucket.
  up_category_switch_after: For products the user bought: number of the user's orders with the product's category placed after the last purchase of the product and the number of distinct other products of that category bought since (evidence of switching to a substitute).
  up_gap_regularity: How regular the user's repurchase gaps for the product are (coefficient of variation of gaps), and days since the user's last purchase divided by the product's typical 90-day median repurchase gap across all buyers.
  up_rebuy_freq_bucket: Pseudo-label rate learned at A-14d per product: among users with the same number of prior distinct orders containing the product (1, 2, 3-4, 5-7, 8+) as this user now, the share whose first order in the next 14 days contained it, smoothed toward the bucket-wide rate.
  up_price_vs_paid_v2: Clipped log ratio of the product's current 7-day median price at the user's main retailer (global 7-day median as fallback) to the price the user paid at the last purchase, guarded against zero prices.
  retailer_adopt14: Pseudo-label adoption rate at A-14d at the user's main retailer: share of returning users whose first order in the last 14 days was at that retailer, had never bought the product before, and added it in that order.
  last_retailer_fit: Fit of the candidate to the retailer of the user's latest order: share of the user's purchases of the product made at that retailer, and log distinct buyers of the product at that retailer in the last 7 days (current availability).
  last_order_copurchase: Item-to-item affinity between the candidate and the products of the user's latest order: the mean over those products of P(candidate in an order | product in that order) over the last 7 days, and its log lift over the candidate's base order rate.
  up_gap_conditioned_share: Smoothed share of the user's orders placed after a gap at least as long as the current days since the user's last order that contain the product, matching the situation of the next order (big restock vs top-up).
  user_parent_category_affinity: Coarser category affinity via the parent of the product's category: share of the user's orders with any product of that parent category and days since the user last bought it, covering never-bought candidates.
  p_basket_context: Order context of the product over the last 28 days: smoothed mean basket size of orders containing it relative to the overall mean (weekly-restock staple vs small-basket item), and the smoothed share of its orders that were top-ups placed within 2 days of the buyer's previous order.
  up_last_quantity_rel: Quantity the user took of the product in the latest purchase relative to the user's mean per-order quantity of it, since a stock-up delays the next repurchase.
