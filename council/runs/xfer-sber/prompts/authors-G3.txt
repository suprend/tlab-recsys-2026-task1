TASK
Orders of a grocery delivery service. Every anchor A is a midnight. A query is a user with at least one order
before A and at least one order in [A, A + 14 days). The target of a query is the set of distinct products of the
user's first order in that window (earliest ts, then smallest order_id); products the user bought before count too.
The label of a (user, product) candidate pair is whether the product is in the target. The metric is MAP@50:
AP@50 = (1 / min(50, M)) * sum over positions k = 1..50 of rel_k * P@k, where M is the number of target products
(targets outside the candidates included), rel_k is 1 when the product at position k is a target, and P@k is the
number of targets among the first k positions divided by k; MAP@50 is the mean AP@50 over all queries.

CANDIDATES (policy S-R150-P100-G200)
Up to 200 candidates per query, all statistics from the history before A.
Main retailer of the user: the retailer with the most distinct orders of the user; ties by the later last order of the user there, then by retailer code.
(a) repeat: the distinct products the user bought before A, by the number of distinct orders with the product descending, then the time of the latest such order descending, then product_id; the first 150.
(b) pop-retailer: products by distinct buyers at the main retailer in [A - 28 days, A) descending, then product_id; the first 100.
(c) global: products by distinct buyers over all orders in [A - 28 days, A) descending, then product_id.
Union: (a) in its order, then (b) without (a), then (c) without (a) and (b), until exactly 200 candidates. Source ranks repeat_rank, popr_rank and glob_rank are positions in the full orders.

TRAINING
CatBoost Logloss, 500 trees, depth 8, learning rate 0.05, L2 10, 128
borders, no tuning. Training anchors are 14 days apart; each contributes at most 20000 queries: all positive pairs
plus 20 hash-sampled negatives per query, with inverse-inclusion weights. Candidates are ranked by the raw
model value, ties by product_id.
